# Polystick reference analysis: "Could Batman Beat Wonder of U"

Reference: 63.1 s, 720×1280, 60 fps, 44 shots. Every second was reviewed
(2 fps labelled sheets for the whole video, full-resolution key frames for
models, 10 fps runs for the two biggest effect sequences). Reference frames
are not stored in the repo.

What could not be verified: the audio content (music, sound effects). Only
its level was measured (mean −17 dB, peak −3 dB); I can't hear it.

## 1. Script (read from the captions)

> Could Batman beat Wonder of U? Wonder of U, from JoJo's Bizarre Adventure,
> is a calamity that hits anyone who goes after its user, Tooru, with a
> meteor falling out of nowhere, and just wanting to catch him is enough to set
> it off. Batman is the world's greatest detective, and his power is
> preparation, with a plan for beating almost anyone, even the Justice League.
> Tooru himself stays in the Batcave and sends drones and the Batmobile on
> autopilot, but the calamity doesn't care who's driving. It cares who wants
> to catch him, and that's Batman, sitting in a cave full of hanging rocks,
> giant computers, and actual bats. A stalactite drops onto the keyboard, the
> screens burst into sparks, and a thousand bats take off at once. Batman
> works out the rule faster than anyone, and the answer is simple: stop
> wanting to catch him. But never giving up is his whole personality, and he's
> already pulling up the backup plan. Alfred finds him next, under a pile of
> broken computers, still holding Tooru's file with one question circled at
> the top.

(Some words are inferred where the caption text dropped a phrase.)

Structure (~150 words): hook question → matchup rules → opponent's power →
hero's strength → "but the rule doesn't care" → escalation to a physical
accident → hero solves it → hero's flaw undoes it → cliffhanger. The rule of
three appears in "hanging rocks, giant computers, actual bats".

## 4. Model and art style

**Overall look.** Smooth-shaded 3D, **no ink outlines**. Soft key light with a
coloured rim light (blue in the cave), gentle bloom, soft shadows. Colour
grade changes per set: deep blue Batcave, warm cream café, desaturated
brown-grey street, pink-lit office.

**Proportions.** Tall, slim, slightly stylised: head ~1/5 of height, a long
neck (the cowl's neck is a black tube), narrow shoulders, upper arms thicker
than forearms. Heads are rounded, with ears, brow ridges and a visible jaw.

**Hands.** Real hands with **four separate fingers and a thumb**, in poses
(relaxed on knees, on a joystick, gripping a clipboard). Our blocks for
hands are the largest single gap.

**Faces.** A painted face texture with eye whites, thick upper lids, lashes
or heavy eyeliner on some characters, a mouth with teeth when angry, and a
large library of expressions: sleepy half-lids, wide shock, dizzy spirals,
big grin, red "rage" face with clenched teeth (a red half-face decal), wink,
tiny pupil dots. The eye look direction moves.

**Costumes.** Detail through colour blocking and decals: bat emblem on the
chest, yellow belt, black gloves and boots, chest highlights, seam lines on
sleeves, cape with simple folds. The target has a spiral-pattern cap, striped
sleeves, vine-print trousers, sun emblem. Costumes are low-polygon but
carefully designed; this is where the "model" feels rich.

**Cape.** A single curved sheet with a slight outward curve; it doesn't
simulate, but it moves a little when the hero moves.

**Sets** are densely dressed:
- *Batcave:* curved console with keyboard and joystick, many screens with UI
  (waveforms, silhouettes, red and blue frames), stalactites, waterfall,
  glowing blue trim on platforms, drone shelves, Batmobile, hologram stage.
- *Street:* block buildings with windows, parked cars, bollards, green bike
  lane, crosswalk, small NPCs (guard, bystanders).
- *Café:* checker floor, big wood-framed windows, pale skyline, hanging lamp,
  table with cup and menu.
- *Office:* blinds with the night city behind, wooden desk.

**Hologram look:** translucent purple, wireframe mesh with a fresnel glow,
used for the "calamity" ghost.

**Particles and atmosphere:** floating golden dust motes in the last scene,
soft beige smoke billows, flat bat silhouettes (hundreds), debris rocks.

`Captions.tsx` already matches this.

## 6. Camera language

- **Shot size cycle:** wide → medium → close-up inside one beat.
- **Moves:** slow push-in (~5–10 %) on holds; short orbits (±15°) on reveals;
  handheld-style drift on close-ups; top-down crane for the meteor; low
  tracking shot on the walking target; tilt up to the cave ceiling; over-the-
  shoulder and behind-the-back for "looking at the wall".
- **Angles vary every cut:** never the same angle twice in a row; high and low
  alternate.
- **Impacts:** camera shake for a few frames, brief zoom, bright flash.
- **Depth:** foreground objects (chair, console, joystick) are kept in frame
  so close-ups feel layered; background is slightly softer than the subject.

## 7. Gap analysis against our engine

| Area | Reference | Ours now | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faces | Painted, many expressions, teeth, lashes, spirals | One line mouth, dot eyes, ~6 states | Expression library (see below) |
| Hands | Fingers, several poses | Single block | Four-finger hand rig + pose presets |
| Costume | Emblems, belts, gloves, boots, seams | Flat colour | Decal/emblem system, trim pieces |
| Cape | Curved sheet that follows | Stiff cone | Segmented cape with sine sway |
| Lighting | Key + coloured rim + bloom + grade per set | One neutral rig | Per-set light presets, bloom, vignette |
| Sets | Densely dressed | Sparse | Set-dressing kits (see below) |
| Camera | Varied move per shot | Mostly one push-in | Move library with seeded drift/shake |
| Effects | ~20 types, tightly timed | ~8 | Effects kit (see below) |
| Pace | 1.43 s average | ~3.3 s average | Shot list targets 1.2–1.6 s |
| Outlines | None | None | Keep it that way: don't add outlines |
