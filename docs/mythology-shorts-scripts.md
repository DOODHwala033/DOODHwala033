# 10 mythology "How X Actually Works !" Shorts scripts
Format: one myth, one real scientific mechanism, a twist on the last line. About 30-35 s each at narrator speed (3.7 words/s).

## 1. How Thor's Lightning Actually Works !

```
Thor doesn't throw lightning. He catches it. A storm cloud builds up a huge electric charge. Then a faint path of charge, called a leader, creeps toward the ground in short steps. When it gets close, sparks reach up from tall, pointed things to meet it. A raised metal hammer is exactly that. The bolt carries around thirty thousand amps and heats the air to about thirty thousand degrees. That's five times hotter than the surface of the Sun. Thunder is just that air exploding outward. So Thor isn't the god of lightning. He's the worst person on Earth to hold a metal hammer in a storm.
```

107 words, about 29 s.

## 2. How Medusa's Gaze Actually Works !

```
Medusa turns people to stone. And in Tanzania, a lake does almost the same thing. Lake Natron is nearly as alkaline as ammonia, and its water can reach sixty degrees. Birds and bats that land in it die. But they don't rot. The salts, called natron, dry the bodies out and stop bacteria. The animals keep their shape, coated in a pale crust that looks exactly like stone. Photographers found them along the shore, and they look like statues. They're not stone, though. They're mummies. So Medusa isn't a monster. She's a very dangerous lake with snakes for hair.
```

99 words, about 27 s.

## 3. How Icarus's Wings Actually Work !

```
Icarus flew too close to the Sun. But the Sun wasn't the problem. The higher you fly, the colder it gets, about six degrees for every kilometre. Beeswax melts at around sixty-two degrees. A few kilometres up, it's already below freezing. And above eight thousand meters, the air is too thin to breathe. Climbers call it the death zone. So long before the wax could melt, Icarus would have frozen, blacked out, and fallen. The Sun didn't bring him down. Altitude did.
```

82 words, about 22 s.

## 4. How Achilles' Heel Actually Works !

```
Achilles had one weak spot. And it's the strongest tendon in your body. Your Achilles tendon is the thickest one you have. Running loads it with up to ten times your body weight. But it has a weakness: very little blood supply. Less blood means slow repair, so tiny tears pile up with every step. That's why it's the most commonly ruptured tendon in the body. In the myth, one arrow takes down the greatest warrior of Troy. In real life, he wouldn't need an arrow. Just a few thousand sprints.
```

91 words, about 25 s.

## 5. How Midas's Golden Touch Actually Works !

```
King Midas turns everything he touches to gold. Physics says that's a death sentence. Gold is nineteen times denser than water. Your body is mostly water, and an adult weighs about seventy kilograms. Turn that body to solid gold, same size, and it weighs thirteen hundred kilograms. That's heavier than a small car. His throne would crack. His floor would collapse. He'd fall straight through his own palace. The curse didn't starve him. It crushed him.
```

76 words, about 21 s.

## 6. How Pegasus Actually Flies !

```
A horse can't fly. And biology has the receipts. The heaviest flying birds weigh around twenty kilograms. A horse weighs about five hundred. Wings have to lift your whole body, and the heavier you are, the harder that gets. The biggest flying animal ever, Quetzalcoatlus, weighed about two hundred and fifty kilograms, with wings eleven to thirteen meters across. And it had hollow bones. A horse has solid muscle and dense bones. So Pegasus can fly, but only if he stops being a horse.
```

84 words, about 23 s.

## 7. How Hades' Helm of Invisibility Actually Works !

```
Hades' helmet makes you invisible. And completely blind. To see, light has to enter your eye and hit your retina. If you're truly invisible, light passes straight through you. Nothing gets absorbed, so no image forms. Scientists call it the reciprocity problem: a perfect cloak can't let you look out. In two thousand six, Duke engineers hid a small object from microwaves by bending them around it. Visible light is far harder. So if Perseus really wore that helm, he didn't sneak up on anyone. He walked into a wall.
```

90 words, about 24 s.

## 8. How Poseidon's Trident Actually Works !

```
Poseidon doesn't make waves. He moves the whole ocean. When an earthquake shifts the seafloor, it lifts the entire column of water above it. That energy spreads out as a tsunami. In deep water, it travels about seven hundred kilometres an hour, as fast as a jet. But the wave is less than a metre high. Ships don't even feel it. Then it reaches shallow water and slows down, and all that energy piles up into a wall. Poseidon's real trident is a tiny ripple that crosses an ocean.
```

89 words, about 24 s.

## 9. How the Hydra Actually Works !

```
The Hydra grows back its heads. And a real Hydra is alive today. It's a tiny freshwater animal, about a centimetre long, named after the monster. Cut it into pieces, and each piece grows into a whole new animal within days. The secret is stem cells. Almost every cell in its body is replaced every few weeks, so damage rarely builds up. Scientists say it barely ages at all. Heracles needed fire to stop the heads. Scientists just want to ask how it does it.
```

85 words, about 23 s.

## 10. How Anubis Actually Weighs Your Heart !

```
In ancient Egypt, your heart had to weigh less than a feather. Anubis puts your heart on one side of the scale, and the feather of Maat on the other. A real human heart weighs about three hundred grams. A feather weighs less than one. So by weight, nobody could ever pass. But the test wasn't about weight. The Egyptians believed your heart held your memories and your deeds. Every lie made it heavier. That's why they left the heart inside the mummy, and threw the brain away.
```

88 words, about 24 s.
